# LabyrinthXR

A cozy VR wooden tilt-maze for Meta Quest 3. Tilt the board with two handles and guide the steel ball past the holes
to the green goal, through five levels: First Steps, Finding Balance, Steady Hands, The Gauntlet, Master of the Maze.
Play with the Touch controllers, or put them down and work the right handle with your bare right hand.

Built with Unity 6000.4.7f1 (built-in render pipeline) on OpenXR, with XR Hands for hand tracking. The ball rolls by
Unity physics; sounds are synthesised at runtime and the wood textures are generated by the scene setup (no audio
or image files).

## Play on the Quest

1. Build: `scripts/labyrinth.sh build` (close the Unity Editor first) → `UnityProject/Builds/LabyrinthXR-quest.apk`.
2. Connect the headset over USB (developer mode, allow USB debugging), then `scripts/labyrinth.sh install` and
   `scripts/labyrinth.sh launch`. Put the headset on first: the Quest refuses to start a VR app while it has no
   6DoF tracking (it shows a "requires 6DoF" dialog instead).
3. Stand up; the labyrinth appears ~0.8 m in front of you at table height. Hold the Meta button to recenter (the
   labyrinth moves in front of you again).

Controls with the Touch controllers:
- **Left hand** – reach for the left red knob and squeeze **grip**: push away / pull back tilts the board forward / back.
- **Right hand** – the right knob: push right / left tilts the board right / left.
- A released handle stays where you left it. Both hands work at the same time; each hand only works its own handle.
- Reaching the goal completes the level; after ~2.5 s the next one loads. Falling restarts the current level.
- **A** or **X** – restart the current level (after Level 5: play again from Level 1).
- **Hold B or Y** (1.5 s) – back to Level 1.
- The current level is shown on the walnut plaque on the front of the frame.

Bare right hand (experimental): turn on hand tracking and automatic hand/controller switching in the Quest settings,
then put both controllers down.
- **Pinch** thumb and index finger near the right knob (closes below ~3.1 cm between the fingertips) and move your
  hand sideways; the handle follows your palm. Open the hand (above ~4.5 cm) to let go; the handle stays put.
- Losing tracking or picking up a controller releases the handle where it is. A hand that comes back already
  pinched does not grab: open and pinch again.
- Coloured debug markers show the tracked palm (blue), thumb tip (red) and index tip (green).
- Only the right handle is hand-driven so far; picking up the left controller switches the Quest back to controllers.

Fit the labyrinth to the player (saved on the headset):
- **Table height:** pinch or grip a brass grip on the table's front edge and move your hand up or down (or right
  stick up/down). 60–110 cm, default 95 cm.
- **Handle spacing:** pinch or grip a handle's wooden base and move your hand sideways; both handles move together
  (or left stick up/down). 30–72 cm apart, default 72 cm.
- **Reset:** hold a fingertip or a controller on the round button in the middle of the table's front edge for 1 s.
- Adjusting lets go of the handles where they are. Desktop: `+`/`-` (PageUp/PageDown) height, `[`/`]` spacing, Home
  reset. See `docs/FEATURE-fit-and-hands.md`.

### Install over wifi

`adb tcpip` once over USB, then `adb connect <headset-ip>:5555` (the headset's address:
`adb shell ip -f inet addr show wlan0`). On macOS the Local Network privacy setting decides whether `adb` may reach
the headset; it follows the app that started the adb server, so if `adb connect` says "No route to host" while
`nc -z <ip> 5555` succeeds, run `adb kill-server; adb connect <ip>:5555` in an app that has Local Network access
(e.g. Terminal) and reuse that server. After a headset restart, repeat the USB step. With both USB and wifi
connected, pick one: `QUEST_DEVICE=<ip>:5555 scripts/labyrinth.sh install`.

## Play in the Unity Editor (no headset)

Open `UnityProject` in Unity 6000.4.7f1, open `Assets/Scenes/Labyrinth.unity`, press Play.
**W/S** (or Up/Down) tilt forward/back, **A/D** (or Left/Right) tilt left/right while held, **R** or **Space**
restarts the level (after Level 5: from Level 1), **1** or **Backspace** goes back to Level 1.

## Development

```sh
scripts/labyrinth.sh test        # EditMode + PlayMode tests (batchmode), watched
scripts/labyrinth.sh snapshots   # renders of the real scene -> UnityProject/Logs/shots/*.png
scripts/labyrinth.sh scene       # regenerate the scene and its generated assets
scripts/labyrinth.sh build       # Quest APK
scripts/labyrinth.sh install | launch | log | devices
```

The device log prefixes hand-tracking events with `[Hands]` (source changes, grabs and releases with their cause,
palm spread per second while held).

See `CLAUDE.md` for architecture and working rules, and the docs for scope and evidence:
- `docs/EPIC-001.md` – the base game: scope, verification log, known limitations.
- `docs/FEATURE-levels.md` – the five levels: design and difficulty measures.
- `docs/DISCOVERY-hand-tracking.md` – the OpenXR switch (Spike 0) and the bare right hand (Spike 1), with test,
  render and Quest 3 results.

Each doc keeps automated tests, off-headset renders, builds and headset playtests apart; only the last one counts as
verified on the Quest.
